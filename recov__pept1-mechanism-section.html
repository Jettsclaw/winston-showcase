The Science

Why your protein takes 3–5 hours.
And ours takes 15 minutes.

It's not about the protein. It's about the transporter. PEPT1 is the body's dedicated fast lane for peptide absorption — and it only accepts di- and tripeptides. Exactly what Recov delivers.

The bottleneck

Whole protein must be enzymatically dismantled — stomach acid, pancreatic enzymes, brush border cleavage — before a single amino acid crosses the intestinal wall.

Every step takes time. Free amino acids then compete for low-capacity, saturable transporters (CAT1, LAT1, ASCT2). High concentrations of one amino acid reduce uptake of others — competitive inhibition. And in the lower gut, bacteria consume amino acids before your body absorbs them. The result: hours of processing, substantial nutrient loss, and a fraction of what you ate reaching your bloodstream.

Non-saturable at physiological doses
PEPT1 has millimolar-range Km values. At normal oral doses, luminal concentrations don't approach saturation. You can take more Recov and it continues absorbing efficiently. Free amino acid transporters have micromolar Km values — they hit a ceiling fast.

Zero competitive inhibition
Free amino acids fight each other for the same transporters. Leucine blocks isoleucine. Valine blocks both. Recov's dipeptides travel as paired units via PEPT1 — no competition. All 20 amino acids arrive together, not one at a time.

Proximal absorption — before bacteria
Whole protein digests through the entire GI tract. In the lower intestine, opportunistic bacteria consume amino acids — especially lysine — before your body does. Recov absorbs in the proximal small intestine, upstream of the bacterial-dense zones.
◆
Preserved liver synthesis capacity
Your liver synthesises ~3g structural protein per hour. Digesting whole protein consumes ~25% of that capacity as thermic overhead. Recov bypasses digestion — PEPT1 transport isn't enzymatic breakdown — so the liver's full capacity stays available for building and repair.
